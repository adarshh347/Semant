# Where Agents Live

When we say that an agent lives inside an image, the phrase risks sounding mystical or merely visual. Where, exactly, is it? Is it a point in pixel space? A process on a server? A character walking through a reconstructed scene?

None of these is quite right.

An agent lives at a **locus**: a declared position in a perceptual world. In the first, strict version, that locus is a particular region of a particular image. It is named by both the post and region identity, because a region called `seg_0` or `arch_2` means nothing by itself; it has a place only within the image that contains it. The agent's position is therefore not a vague attachment to a file. It is an address in a world of durable visual material.

```text
locus = this image + this region + this available body of organs
```

From that address, the agent has a view. It may see relations involving its region. It may not speak as though it has surveyed every other region in the frame. That would be a god's-eye reading, not situated perception.

## The perceptual world is layered

The world an agent inhabits has more than one layer, and the distinction protects both freedom and truth.

The first layer is the material world: images, regions, fields, paths, marks, and grounds. It is where the agent stands and where instruments make contact with pixels.

The second layer is the agent's private world: a percept field, a trajectory, and episodic memory. This is not a second copy of the archive. It is the history of what this particular body encountered from its particular location. Two agents can inhabit different loci or have different organ sets and therefore carry different memories without either one being defective.

The third layer is shared memory: the Atlas of committed marks, relations, movement edges, and axes. This is the world that persists beyond one agent's run. It is not a transcript of every thought or every sensory twitch. It is the carefully attributable record that other agents and curators may rely on.

```text
image material
  → private situated experience
  → proposed report
  → curator-committed shared topology
```

An agent can live on a fresh organ reading before a curator commits it. The archive cannot. This asymmetry is the difference between perception and public belief.

## A world is not a container

It is tempting to imagine all agents gathered inside a single global simulation. Semant takes a more interesting route. A world is not one large container that everybody sees in full. It is a network of accessible loci, evidence, and paths.

An agent may begin in a temple detail, another in a garment fold, another in a chromatic field. They do not become neighbours because their processes are running together. They become neighbours only when a grounded relation opens a horizon between their loci. Until then, their worlds remain separate in the only sense that matters: no honest path connects them.

This makes absence meaningful. A lack of relation is not a failed search to be papered over with a generated bridge. It is the current shape of the world. New perception may change that shape, but language alone cannot.

## What about imagined worlds?

There will eventually be another kind of habitation: a hybrid world assembled from several grounded fragments and the correspondences between them. Such a world may let an agent explore a composed structure—for example, a world of recursive arches, shadows, and reflections.

But it cannot be confused with a photographed world. Its status is imagined. Its bridges must cite the measured relations that allowed the construction. Anything discovered inside it is a hypothesis until a real image grounds it back.

This boundary is not a limitation on creativity. It is what allows creativity to be generous without becoming deceitful. An agent can travel into an imagined world, make conjectures there, and return with questions for the real archive. It cannot smuggle a daydream back as a measurement.

So where do agents live? In a world of locations rather than a world of total views; in a field of evidence rather than a chat history; in a private trajectory that can meet a public topology without being mistaken for it. They live wherever the system can honestly say both *where this agent stands* and *what it has grounds to know from there*.
