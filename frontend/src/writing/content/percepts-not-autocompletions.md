# Percepts Are Not Autocompletions

The most tempting way to build a system that looks at images is also the easiest. Put an image beside a prompt, ask a large model what it sees, and accept the continuation that follows. The result can be startlingly good. It can name materials, moods, architectural lineages, gestures, and relations in sentences that sound as though someone had spent time with the picture.

But a continuation is not yet a percept.

The difference is easy to miss because both may arrive as language. A model says that the ornament draws inward; a curator writes that the arches hold one another; an instrument reports a measured containment relation. Each can be rendered as a sentence. Yet the sentence alone does not tell us what kind of thing it is, where it came from, or what may legitimately be done with it next.

A **percept** is an act of noticing given an identity and a ground. It says not merely *this image suggests inwardness*, but: *this particular noticing rests on these particular pieces of evidence, was made at this time, and can be revisited without asking a model to improvise it again.* That is a very different object from an answer generated afresh each time a question is asked.

> A continuation is language that follows language. A percept is a claim that can point back down to what made it possible.

This is why Semant treats the durable expression percept as small and exact. It has an identity, an expression, a list of ground identifiers, a maker, a time, and—when needed—the roles its grounds play. It does not pretend to contain every possible interpretation of an image. It does not own geometry. Its extent belongs to what it rests on. Its work is to join a sentence of attention to the evidence that lets that sentence be checked.

## The sentence is not the unit

Consider two ways of recording the same encounter with an image.

The first is the usual AI record:

```text
"The repeated arches create a feeling of recursive enclosure."
```

It may be a fine sentence. But if we return tomorrow, we do not know whether it was based on the arches, the crop, a prior about Islamic architecture, or the model's desire to say something coherent. It has no address in the world. It cannot become part of a path through the archive because there is nothing in it to traverse.

The second is a perceptual record:

```text
expression: "The inner arch is nested within the outer arch."
grounds: [the inner region, the outer region]
properties: [containment, repetition]
```

Now the statement can be inspected. A later reader can open the grounds. A geometry instrument can test the relation again. A different percept can cite one of the same grounds and disagree. A movement engine can ask whether the relation also holds elsewhere. The sentence has become a participant in a world rather than a pleasing exhaust plume from one model call.

This does not make every percept objectively final. Perception is not certainty. It makes the uncertainty legible. A curator's expression percept is an authored act of noticing. An organ's observation is constrained by what its instrument measured. A model's suggested wording remains a suggestion until someone accepts it. The important thing is not to flatten these into the same anonymous text field.

## Why identity changes everything

Language without identity has a strange kind of amnesia. Ask twice and it answers twice; the second answer can resemble the first but is not accountable to it. Nothing accumulates except chat history, and chat history is a poor memory for a world because it records sequence without preserving structure.

An identified percept can accumulate relations. It may be cited in prose, linked to a mark, placed on an axis, or compared with another percept across images. It can be accepted, rejected, forked, or relabelled without erasing the fact that it was once made. It gives the system something to remember other than the last words it generated.

That is also why the distinction between a durable percept and a draft matters. A director may propose a sentence of close reading. That proposal can be useful, even beautiful. But it is not silently written into the perceptual world. It is a draft, a precursor to a mark or a curator action, and it remains visibly provisional. Conflating proposal with perception is how a system begins to believe its own autocomplete.

## From answering to noticing

The ambition is not to make a model less eloquent. It is to give eloquence a place in a larger ecology. Language can propose, compose, and help a person see. But the perceptual layer needs objects that survive the moment of speech: objects with evidence, identity, provenance, and the ability to be tested again.

That shift changes the question we ask of AI. Instead of *can it describe this image convincingly?* we ask: *what did it notice, what does the noticing rest on, and what new movement does that make possible?*

Only the second question can lead from one image to another without losing the ground beneath the words.
