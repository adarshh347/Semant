Perceptual movement travels within a field of what has been measured. This essay is about the harder motion: the one that crosses from one image to another and arrives somewhere that neither image contained on its own.

Call the thing that opens between them a **horizon**.

## The example that will not leave me

A Turkish facade, photographed straight on. In the tilework, a superstructure of nested circles — rings within rings, the ornament organising itself by containment.

A Persian interior, photographed from below. The architecture of the room nests too: an alcove within an arch within a vault, each space held inside the next.

Nothing connects these pictures. Different countries, different centuries, different materials, different photographers, different light. An object detector finds tile in one and stone in the other and is right about both and has told you nothing.

And yet: the same *nesting* is happening. Not the same shapes — the same organising principle, working once in two dimensions of surface decoration and once in three dimensions of inhabited space. Recognising this is not classification. It is the opening of a relation that did not exist until both images were held at once, along the right axis, by someone attending to structure rather than to material.

That relation is the horizon. And the claim I want to make is that it should be a **first-class object** in the system — a thing with an identity, a record, and a status — rather than a sentence the model happens to emit.

## Why a sentence is not enough

If the connection lives only in generated prose, three things follow, all bad.

It cannot be checked. The passage from Turkish tile to Persian vault reads as insight or as nonsense depending entirely on how well it is written, and writing quality is exactly the wrong instrument for judging whether a relation holds.

It cannot be revisited. Every asking regenerates it, slightly differently, with no continuity. Nothing accumulates.

And it cannot be wrong in any useful way. A sentence that connects two images is not falsifiable unless you can say what it rests on. Make the horizon an object — a typed relation over specific perceptual loci, carrying the axis it was opened along and the evidence that supports it — and suddenly it can be examined, disputed, strengthened, or retired.

> A connection you cannot inspect is not a connection. It is a performance of one.

## What makes a horizon real

The same discipline that governs perceptual movement governs this, only more strictly, because the temptation is greater. Cross-image resemblance is the easiest thing in the world to hallucinate. Any two images can be related if you are allowed to invent the relation.

So a horizon earns its existence from below. It must rest on what the specialist organs actually measured on each side — the geometric organ finding containment in the tile, the geometric organ finding containment in the vault — and on a relation that is genuinely there, not merely available to a fluent describer. If the organs did not measure nesting on both sides, there is no nesting horizon. There may be something else, or there may be nothing, and *nothing* has to remain a permitted answer.

This is the point where systems usually cheat, and where cheating is invisible, because a fabricated horizon and a real one produce identical prose.

## Living, not dead

There is one more property, and it is the one I find most demanding.

A horizon should stay **living**. The dead version is a cached edge: computed once, stored, retrieved forever. It was true at the moment of computation and thereafter it is a fossil, asserting a relation whose grounds may have changed — new organs may have run, new evidence arrived, the corpus grown.

The living version is re-evaluated. It carries its grounds with it and can lose its standing when those grounds no longer hold. It is less a fact than a *claim under maintenance*.

The distinction generalises past horizons, and it is the sharpest line I know for separating this from ordinary retrieval. Dead means one-shot: query in, answer out, nothing persists. Living means state that endures, memory that accumulates, and relations that continue to be tested after they are first made. Not a system that answers about images, but a field that keeps holding them in relation and notices when a relation stops being true.

## What this is for

The nested circles do not explain the Persian vault. That is not the ambition. The ambition is that when you are looking at one, the other becomes *available* — along a named axis, on stated evidence, in a way you can follow back and refuse.

Most of what we call machine understanding of images is a very good labelling of one picture at a time. What I am after is the movement between pictures, and the insistence that the movement be real: that the horizon which opens between a Turkish facade and a Persian interior is either grounded in what was actually perceived on both sides, or is not offered at all.

Between the two there is no middle option that is honest. There is only the middle option that is fluent.
